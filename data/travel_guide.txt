HOLIDAY & EVERYDAY OFFLINE KNOWLEDGE BASE
==========================================
For use when traveling to remote locations, island resorts, or anywhere without internet.
Covers: Travel, Car Troubles, Health & First Aid, Tech, Food, Conversions, and more.

======================================
SECTION 1: CAR AND VEHICLE PROBLEMS
======================================

Q: How do I jump start a car?
A: Connect the red (positive) cable to the positive terminal (+) of the dead battery. Connect the other red clamp to the positive terminal (+) of the working battery. Connect the black (negative) cable to the negative terminal (-) of the working battery. Connect the final black clamp to an unpainted metal surface on the dead car's engine block (NOT the dead battery). Start the working car, let it run for 2 minutes, then start the dead car. Remove cables in reverse order once the dead car is running.

Q: How do I change a flat tyre?
A: Loosen the lug nuts slightly before jacking the car. Place the jack under the vehicle's reinforced jack point (check your car manual or look for a notch under the door). Jack the car up until the flat tyre is about 15cm off the ground. Remove the lug nuts and pull off the flat tyre. Mount the spare tyre and hand-tighten the lug nuts in a star/cross pattern. Lower the car, then tighten the lug nuts fully. Drive to a garage as soon as possible if you are using a temporary spare.

Q: My car is overheating. What do I do?
A: Pull over safely and turn off the engine immediately. Do NOT open the radiator cap when the engine is hot as boiling coolant will spray out. Wait at least 30 minutes for the engine to cool. Check the coolant level once cool and top it up with water if you have no coolant. Check for obvious leaks. If you must continue driving, turn on the heater (it draws heat away from the engine), and drive slowly. Stop and let the engine cool repeatedly if the temperature gauge rises again.

Q: My car won't start and I can hear a clicking sound. What's wrong?
A: A rapid clicking sound when turning the key usually means a dead or weak battery. A single loud click often means the starter motor is faulty. Try jump starting the car first (see jump start guide above). If jump starting works, your battery is flat and may need replacement. If it doesn't work, you may need a new starter motor or there is a more serious electrical issue.

Q: How do I check my tyre pressure without a gauge?
A: Press your thumb firmly into the tyre sidewall. A properly inflated tyre should feel very firm and barely flex. A soft or spongy feeling means the tyre is underinflated. This is not precise, so check with a gauge at the earliest opportunity. Most passenger cars need 30 to 35 PSI. Driving on significantly underinflated tyres is dangerous and damages the tyre.

Q: How do I know if I am running low on fuel?
A: Most cars have a low fuel warning light (a fuel pump icon). When it lights up, you typically have 50 to 80 km of range remaining, depending on your car. Reserve can vary widely. Drive at a steady moderate speed (not too fast, not too slow) to maximise remaining fuel efficiency. Avoid air conditioning if possible. If you run out of fuel, coast to the roadside safely, hazard lights on.

Q: How do I get out of soft sand if my car is stuck?
A: Do not spin the tyres at high speed as this digs you in deeper. Let air out of all four tyres to about 15 to 20 PSI to increase traction (re-inflate at a petrol station afterward). Try rocking: reverse slightly then drive forward in a quick rhythm to build momentum. Place any flat material (floor mats, branches, clothing) in front of the drive wheels for grip. Get passengers to push from behind while you gently accelerate.

SECTION 2: EVERYDAY HEALTH AND FIRST AID
======================================

Q: How do I remove a splinter?
A: Clean the area with soap and water. Sterilize a needle or tweezers by heating with a flame or rubbing with alcohol. Use the needle to gently break the skin at the point of entry and expose the end of the splinter. Grip with tweezers and pull out at the same angle it went in. Clean the wound again and apply antiseptic. If the splinter is deep, very large, or near the eye, seek medical help.

Q: What are the symptoms of food poisoning?
A: Food poisoning symptoms typically begin 2 to 6 hours after eating contaminated food. They include: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea (sometimes with blood), stomach cramps, and fever. Treatment: rest, drink plenty of fluids to replace what is lost (oral rehydration salts are ideal). Eat bland foods when vomiting stops (toast, rice, banana). Seek medical attention if symptoms include bloody diarrhoea, fever above 38.5°C, severe abdominal pain, or if symptoms last more than 2 to 3 days.

Q: How do I treat mild sunburn?
A: Get out of the sun. Take a cool shower or bath. Apply aloe vera gel or a gentle moisturizing lotion. Take ibuprofen or paracetamol for pain. Drink extra water. Do not pop blisters. Cover burned skin when outdoors. Avoid further sun exposure until fully healed. See a doctor if blisters cover a large area, you feel faint, or have a high fever.

Q: What do I do if someone has an allergic reaction?
A: Mild allergic reaction (hives, itching, mild swelling): antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine) can help. Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis): symptoms include throat swelling, difficulty breathing, rapid heartbeat, and loss of consciousness. This is a life-threatening emergency. Use an EpiPen (adrenaline auto-injector) if available. Lay the person flat and raise their legs. Call emergency services immediately. Even if symptoms improve after EpiPen, the person must go to hospital.

Q: How do I treat a blister from walking?
A: Do not pop the blister if it is intact, as the skin protects against infection. Cover it with a loose bandage or blister plaster. If it is already burst, clean it gently with clean water, trim away dead skin with clean scissors, and cover with an antiseptic dressing. To prevent blisters: wear well-fitting shoes and moisture-wicking socks, apply petroleum jelly or anti-chafe balm to hot spots before walking.

Q: What should I do for a mild headache?
A: Take paracetamol (500mg to 1g) or ibuprofen (200 to 400mg) with food. Drink at least 2 glasses of water as dehydration is a very common cause. Rest in a quiet, dark room. Apply a cold or warm compress to the forehead or neck. Stretch your neck and shoulders gently. See a doctor if the headache is the worst of your life, comes suddenly like a thunderclap, is accompanied by fever and stiff neck, or follows a head injury.

Q: How do I stop a nosebleed?
A: Sit upright and lean slightly forward (not backward; this causes blood to flow down the throat). Pinch the soft lower part of the nose firmly between your thumb and forefinger. Breathe through your mouth. Hold continuously for 10 to 15 minutes without releasing. Apply a cold compress to the bridge of the nose. Seek medical attention if bleeding doesn't stop after 20 minutes or if the nosebleed followed a head injury.

Q: How do I treat a bee or wasp sting?
A: If a bee sting is visible, scrape it off sideways with a credit card or fingernail (do not use tweezers as squeezing may push more venom in). Wash the area with soap and water. Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling and pain. Antihistamine cream or tablets can reduce itching. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain. Watch for signs of anaphylaxis (severe allergy) in the 30 minutes following the sting.

Q: What is the best treatment for diarrhoea when travelling?
A: Drink plenty of clear fluids: water, diluted fruit juice, clear broth. Oral rehydration salts (ORS) are ideal if available. Eat bland, easily digestible foods: bananas, rice, boiled potatoes, toast. Avoid dairy, fatty foods, high-fibre foods, caffeine, and alcohol. Loperamide (Imodium) can reduce the frequency of diarrhoea but does not treat the underlying cause. See a doctor if diarrhoea is bloody, accompanied by high fever, or lasts more than 3 days.

Q: How do I treat a mild burn from a stove or hot object?
A: Immediately cool the burn under cool (not ice cold) running water for at least 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter, toothpaste, or any home remedy. Cover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy cloth. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain. Do not pop blisters. Go to hospital if the burn is larger than the palm of your hand, if it is on the face, hands, feet, genitals, or a joint, or if the skin looks white, brown, or black.

Q: What do I do about jet lag?
A: Adjust your sleep schedule to the local time zone as quickly as possible. Get exposure to natural daylight at appropriate times (morning light helps if you arrived going east, afternoon light helps going west). Stay hydrated and avoid alcohol and caffeine in the first day. Melatonin supplements (0.5 to 5mg taken at bedtime in the new time zone) can help reset your internal clock. Avoid long naps during the day at your destination.

SECTION 3: TECHNOLOGY TROUBLESHOOTING
======================================

Q: My laptop won't turn on. What do I check first?
A: Check if the battery is completely drained: plug in the charger and wait 10 to 15 minutes before pressing power. Try a different power outlet or check if a fuse has tripped. Hold the power button for 30 seconds while unplugged to discharge any residual power, then plug in and try again. If a laptop makes no sound and shows no lights at all, the battery may have died completely or the charger is faulty. Check if the charger LED is lit when plugged in.

Q: My phone has no signal in a remote area. How do I still use it?
A: Enable Wi-Fi calling if your carrier supports it and there is a Wi-Fi connection nearby. Download maps offline before travel (Google Maps allows offline area downloads). Use SMS text messages which often work at lower signal strengths where calls fail. Enable airplane mode and then re-enable mobile data to force the phone to search for the best available signal. Move to higher ground as signal is generally better at elevation.

Q: How do I save my phone battery when no charging is available?
A: Enable low power / battery saver mode. Reduce screen brightness to the minimum you can comfortably see. Turn off Bluetooth, Wi-Fi (if not using), location services, and background app refresh. Enable airplane mode if you do not need calls or data, as searching for signal drains battery quickly. Close all background apps. Reduce screen timeout to 15 to 30 seconds. Keeping the phone in a cool, dark place also helps battery efficiency.

Q: My phone got wet. What should I do?
A: Turn it off immediately. Do not charge it. Remove the SIM card and any memory cards. Pat dry with a clean cloth gently. Do not use a hairdryer as heat can damage components. Place the phone in a bag of dry uncooked rice or silica gel sachets for 24 to 48 hours to absorb moisture. Do not turn it on for at least 24 hours. Modern flagship phones often have water resistance ratings but still avoid submerging or exposing to saltwater.

Q: The Wi-Fi connection is very slow at my hotel or resort. How do I fix it?
A: Move closer to the router or Wi-Fi access point. Disconnect from Wi-Fi and reconnect. Forget the network and rejoin. Restart your device. Try connecting to a different frequency band if available (5GHz is faster but shorter range; 2.4GHz is slower but longer range). Reduce video streaming quality. Avoid peak evening hours when many guests are online. Use a VPN as it can sometimes improve speeds on throttled connections.

Q: How do I extend the range of a weak Wi-Fi signal?
A: Move your device to a location between the router and your current position. Ensure there are no thick walls, large appliances, or metal objects blocking the signal path. If at a resort, connect to the closest outdoor access point rather than relying on one router to cover the entire property. Some smartphones can be used as Wi-Fi repeaters in hotspot mode.

Q: How do I connect my laptop to mobile hotspot?
A: On your phone, go to Settings > Mobile Hotspot (or Tethering). Enable the hotspot and note the network name and password shown. On your laptop, click the Wi-Fi icon, search for available networks, select your phone's hotspot name, and enter the password. To save your phone battery while hotspotting, connect only essential devices and limit streaming.

Q: How do I convert common measurements?
A: TEMPERATURE: Celsius to Fahrenheit = (C × 9/5) + 32. Fahrenheit to Celsius = (F - 32) × 5/9. Common references: 0°C = 32°F (freezing), 20°C = 68°F (comfortable), 30°C = 86°F (hot), 37°C = 98.6°F (body temperature), 100°C = 212°F (boiling).
DISTANCE: 1 km = 0.621 miles. 1 mile = 1.609 km. 1 meter = 3.28 feet. 1 foot = 30.48 cm.
WEIGHT: 1 kg = 2.205 pounds. 1 pound = 0.454 kg.
VOLUME: 1 liter = 0.264 US gallons. 1 US gallon = 3.785 liters.
CURRENCY: Always check current exchange rates before travel. Keep a note of approximate rates offline.

Q: How do I handle a medical emergency in a foreign country?
A: Save the local emergency number before you arrive (not all countries use 112 or 911). Know the name and address of the nearest hospital to your accommodation. Keep your travel insurance card and policy number accessible. If hospitalized, contact your travel insurance company's 24-hour helpline as soon as possible. Your embassy or high commission can also assist citizens in medical emergencies abroad.

Q: What should I keep in a travel first aid kit?
A: Paracetamol and ibuprofen (pain and fever). Antihistamines (cetirizine for allergies). Oral rehydration salts (diarrhoea and dehydration). Antidiarrhoeal tablets (loperamide). Antiseptic wipes and cream. Adhesive plasters and bandages. Blister plasters. Tweezers. Small scissors. Sunscreen (SPF 50+). Insect repellent (DEET-based). Any personal prescription medications.

Q: How do I deal with motion sickness?
A: Take motion sickness medication (cinnarizine or meclizine) 30 to 60 minutes before travel. Sit in the front of a car, the middle of a boat, or over the wing of a plane where motion is least. Look at the horizon or a fixed point in the distance. Avoid reading or looking at screens while moving. Keep fresh air flowing. Eat light, bland food before travel. Avoid alcohol and strong smells. Ginger (tea or chews) is a well-evidenced natural remedy.

Q: How do I protect my skin from the sun while holidaying?
A: Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen 20 minutes before sun exposure and reapply every 2 hours and after swimming. Cover up with lightweight long-sleeved clothing, a wide-brimmed hat, and UV-protective sunglasses. Seek shade between 10am and 4pm when UV radiation is strongest. Be aware that you can burn on cloudy days, in shallow water, and near reflective surfaces like sand and sea. Sunscreen degrades in heat so store it in a cool, shaded place.

Q: What should I do if my passport is lost or stolen abroad?
A: Report the loss immediately to the local police and get a written police report (needed for insurance and embassy). Contact your country's nearest embassy or consulate with your ID and police report. They can issue an Emergency Travel Document (ETD) to get you home. Keep a digital and physical copy of your passport separately from the original to speed up this process. Travel insurance often covers costs related to document replacement.

Q: How do I stay safe from petty theft while travelling?
A: Keep your bag in front of you in crowded places. Use a money belt worn under clothing for your passport and large amounts of cash. Split your cash and cards between different locations on your body. Use hotel safe boxes for passports and extra cash. Be aware of distraction tactics used by pickpockets. Avoid displaying expensive cameras, phones, or jewellery conspicuously. Research common scams at your destination before you arrive.

SECTION 5: FOOD AND COOKING TIPS
======================================

Q: How do I tell if food has gone bad?
A: Trust your senses. Bad smell is the most reliable indicator. Visible mould, unusual colour, or slimy texture are clear signs of spoilage. Dairy products that have soured and meat that has changed to grey or green are unsafe. When in doubt, throw it out. The golden rule: heat cannot destroy all toxins. Food that smells and looks bad is NOT made safe by cooking. Some bacteria produce toxins that remain dangerous even after boiling.

Q: What is the correct way to defrost meat safely?
A: The safest method is to defrost in the refrigerator (slow and controlled). Place frozen meat in a bowl on the bottom shelf to prevent dripping on other foods. Allow 24 hours per 2kg. For faster defrosting, submerge sealed meat in cold running water (not warm) and change the water every 30 minutes. Never defrost at room temperature as the outer layers warm to unsafe temperatures while the center is still frozen. Cook immediately after defrosting with either method.

Q: How do I cook rice perfectly?
A: Rinse rice until the water runs clear to remove excess starch. Standard ratio: 1 cup rice to 1.5 cups water (white rice) or 1 cup rice to 2 cups water (brown rice). Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to the lowest setting, cover tightly, and cook for 18 minutes (white) or 45 minutes (brown). Do not lift the lid during cooking. After cooking, remove from heat and let stand covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. Leftover rice must be cooled quickly and refrigerated; reheating incorrectly is a common cause of food poisoning.

Q: What are good foods to eat when you have an upset stomach?
A: The BRAT diet is recommended: Bananas, Rice (white), Applesauce, and Toast. These are bland, easy to digest, and gentle on an irritated stomach. Also consider plain boiled potatoes, clear broth, plain crackers, and boiled chicken without skin. Avoid: spicy foods, dairy, fatty or fried foods, raw vegetables, caffeine, and alcohol until fully recovered. Reintroduce normal foods gradually once symptoms have settled.

Q: How do I remove common food stains from clothing?
A: Red wine: blot immediately with cold water, apply salt to absorb remaining liquid, then rinse. Tomato sauce: scrape off excess, rinse with cold water from the back of the fabric, apply dish soap. Oil/grease: apply dish soap or corn starch immediately to absorb oil, then wash in the hottest water safe for the fabric. Coffee: rinse with cold water immediately, then apply a mixture of dish soap and white vinegar. Ink: apply rubbing alcohol or hand sanitizer before washing. Never use hot water first on protein stains (blood, egg) as it sets them.

SECTION 6: GENERAL PRACTICAL KNOWLEDGE
======================================

Q: How do I reset a router or modem?
A: Find the reset button (usually a small hole at the back or bottom of the device). Use a pin or paperclip to press and hold the reset button for 10 to 30 seconds until the lights blink or change. Wait 2 minutes for the device to fully restart. The router will return to its factory default settings, so you will need to set it up again with your ISP's settings. Before resetting, try simply unplugging the device for 30 seconds and plugging back in, which may solve connectivity issues without a full reset.

Q: How do I calculate a tip at a restaurant?
A: A quick mental method: for 10%, move the decimal point one place left (e.g. a bill of ₹800 → 10% tip = ₹80). For 15%, find 10% then add half. For 20%, find 10% then double it. Standard tipping etiquette varies by country. In India and Southeast Asia, tipping is appreciated but not mandatory. In the USA, 15 to 20% is standard. In Japan, tipping is considered rude. In Europe, rounding up the bill or leaving 5 to 10% is common.

Q: How do I know if a bill (banknote) is fake?
A: Hold the note up to light: genuine notes have a watermark (image visible when held to light) and a security thread (embedded metallic strip). Feel the texture: genuine notes often have raised print that feels slightly rough. Check for color-shifting ink on some denominations (the number changes color when you tilt the note). Use a UV light: genuine notes often have fluorescent patterns invisible in normal light. When in doubt, compare with a note you know is genuine.

Q: How do I manage my sleep schedule when travelling across time zones?
A: Before departure: gradually shift your sleep and wake times toward the destination's time zone (15 to 30 minutes per day). On the plane: set your watch to the destination's time zone immediately. Sleep only when it will be nighttime at your destination. Avoid alcohol on the flight as it disrupts sleep quality. On arrival: get outdoor light exposure in the morning to reset your body clock. Stay awake until local bedtime on the first night even if exhausted.

Q: How do I pack efficiently for a trip?
A: Roll clothes instead of folding to save space and reduce wrinkles. Pack versatile clothing that can be mixed and matched. Use packing cubes to organize and compress clothing. Place heaviest items close to your back in a backpack or at the bottom of a suitcase. Pack shoes in shower caps to keep them separate. Keep all liquids in a clear zip-lock bag. Pack a change of clothes in your carry-on in case checked luggage is delayed. Leave room for items purchased during your trip.

Q: How do I avoid getting sick from water when travelling?
A: In areas with unsafe tap water: drink only bottled water, boiled water, or water purified with iodine tablets or a certified filter. Avoid ice in drinks unless you know it is made from purified water. Brush teeth with bottled or boiled water. Be cautious with raw salads and fruits washed in tap water. Carbonated water is generally safer than still as the carbonation process reduces risk. Water that has been boiled and allowed to cool in a clean, covered container is safe to drink.

Q: How do I make a collect/reverse-charge call from abroad?
A: Dial your country's international access code, then the number you want to reach. For a collect call (where the recipient pays), you typically need to call an operator. In many countries, you can dial the international operator by dialing 0 or 00. With modern smartphones, international calling apps (WhatsApp, FaceTime, Skype) over Wi-Fi are often the easiest free alternative when you have internet access.
