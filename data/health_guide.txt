HEALTH, FITNESS AND BODY OFFLINE GUIDE
=======================================
Common health questions answered for when you have no internet access.

======================================
SECTION 1: COMMON ILLNESSES AND SYMPTOMS
======================================

Q: How do I know if I have a cold or the flu?
A: Cold symptoms come on gradually and include runny nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, and mild fatigue. Fever is rare with a cold. Flu symptoms come on suddenly and are much more severe: high fever (38 to 40°C), intense body aches, severe fatigue, headache, dry cough, and chills. The golden rule: if a 100-rupee note was on the floor and you feel too bad to pick it up, it is likely flu, not a cold. Treatment for both is rest, fluids, and paracetamol for fever and aches.

Q: I have a sore throat. What helps?
A: Gargle with warm salt water (1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon salt in 8 oz warm water) every few hours. Drink warm liquids: tea with honey, warm water with honey and lemon. Suck on throat lozenges. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain. Use a humidifier if the air is dry. See a doctor if: the sore throat is severe, lasts more than a week, is accompanied by high fever, difficulty swallowing, or if you see white patches on the tonsils (could be strep throat or tonsillitis requiring antibiotics).

Q: How do I know if a wound is infected?
A: Signs of an infected wound include: increasing redness spreading outward from the wound, warmth around the wound, swelling, pus (yellow or green discharge), increasing pain (rather than decreasing as the wound heals), fever, and red streaks extending from the wound (a sign of serious spreading infection). A clean healing wound should show decreasing pain, edges coming together, and no discharge. Treat infected wounds immediately. If red streaks appear, seek emergency medical care.

Q: What do I do for a bad headache that won't go away?
A: Try paracetamol (1g) or ibuprofen (400mg) with a large glass of water. Lie down in a dark, quiet room. Apply a cold or warm compress to the forehead or neck. Try gentle neck stretches. Avoid screens. If you regularly get severe headaches, keep a diary of triggers (certain foods, dehydration, stress, lack of sleep, caffeine withdrawal). See a doctor urgently if the headache: is the worst of your life, starts suddenly like an explosion, is accompanied by fever, stiff neck, or rash, follows a head injury, or is accompanied by vision changes or weakness.

Q: What causes frequent headaches and how do I prevent them?
A: Common causes: dehydration (drink 2 to 3 liters of water daily), tension headache from muscle tightness (take breaks from screens, stretch neck and shoulders regularly), caffeine withdrawal (cutting back on coffee or tea too quickly), poor sleep, skipping meals causing low blood sugar, eye strain (check if you need glasses or an updated prescription), and stress. Prevention: regular sleep schedule, adequate hydration, regular meals, screen breaks every hour using the 20-20-20 rule (look 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes).

Q: I have a rash. How do I know if it is serious?
A: See a doctor immediately for: a rash with fever and stiff neck (could be meningitis), a rash that looks like a bull's eye (Lyme disease), a spreading rash accompanied by difficulty breathing or throat swelling (anaphylaxis), a rash covering large areas of the body or causing blistering, or a rash that appears suddenly with joint pain and fever. Less urgent rashes (hives, heat rash, contact dermatitis) can be treated with antihistamines and by avoiding the trigger. Apply calamine lotion or hydrocortisone cream to calm itching.

Q: What is the correct dose of common over-the-counter medicines?
A: PARACETAMOL (adults): 500mg to 1000mg every 4 to 6 hours. Maximum 4000mg (4g) per day. Never exceed this dose as it can cause liver damage.
IBUPROFEN (adults): 200mg to 400mg every 4 to 6 hours with food. Maximum 1200mg per day for self-medication. Do not take on an empty stomach or if you have stomach ulcers.
CETIRIZINE (antihistamine): 10mg once daily.
LOPERAMIDE (anti-diarrhoeal): 4mg initially then 2mg after each loose stool. Maximum 8mg per day.
ASPIRIN: 300 to 600mg every 4 to 6 hours. Do not give to children under 16.

Q: How do I treat a muscle cramp?
A: Stretch the cramped muscle immediately. For a calf cramp (the most common): straighten the leg and flex your foot upward toward your shin. Massage the cramping muscle firmly. Apply heat to relax the muscle (warm cloth or hot water bottle). Walk around slowly. Drink water and consider electrolyte drinks as cramps are often related to dehydration and low mineral levels (magnesium, potassium, sodium). Frequent cramps may indicate poor circulation or mineral deficiency.

Q: How do I reduce swelling from an injury quickly?
A: Apply the RICE method: Rest (stop activity), Ice (apply ice pack wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes every hour in the first 24 to 48 hours, never apply ice directly to skin), Compression (firm bandage wrap), Elevation (raise the injured part above the level of the heart). After 48 hours, switch from ice to heat to improve blood flow and promote healing. Anti-inflammatory medication (ibuprofen) taken with food also reduces swelling.

Q: How do I know if someone is having a stroke?
A: Use the FAST test: Face drooping (ask them to smile, does one side droop?), Arm weakness (ask them to raise both arms, does one drift downward?), Speech slurred or strange (ask them to repeat a simple sentence), Time to call emergency services immediately. Other stroke signs: sudden severe headache with no known cause, sudden vision problems, sudden severe dizziness, and loss of coordination. Every minute counts in a stroke. Brain cells die rapidly without treatment. Act FAST.

Q: What are the signs of a heart attack?
A: Classic symptoms: chest pain or pressure (often described as a heavy weight or squeezing), pain radiating to the left arm, jaw, neck, or back, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea and vomiting, and light-headedness. Call emergency services immediately. Chew (do not swallow whole) a full-dose aspirin (300mg) if available and not allergic, as it helps prevent further clotting. Keep the person calm, sitting up, and loosening any tight clothing. Be prepared to perform CPR if they become unresponsive.

SECTION 2: FITNESS AND EXERCISE
======================================

Q: How do I build muscle without gym equipment?
A: The most effective bodyweight exercises are: push-ups (chest, shoulders, triceps), pull-ups (back, biceps), squats (legs, glutes), lunges (legs, glutes), dips on a chair (triceps), planks (core), burpees (full body), and mountain climbers (core, cardio). Progressive overload is key: make exercises harder over time by adding reps, sets, slowing down the movement, or choosing harder variations. Aim for 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 15 repetitions per exercise. Rest 48 hours between training the same muscle group.

Q: How do I lose weight effectively?
A: Weight loss comes down to a calorie deficit (consuming fewer calories than you burn). Eat more whole foods (vegetables, lean protein, whole grains) which are filling but lower in calories. Reduce ultra-processed foods, sugary drinks, and alcohol. Do not skip meals as it often leads to overeating later. Aim for 0.5 to 1 kg loss per week (sustainable rate). Exercise increases calorie burn and preserves muscle. Sleep 7 to 9 hours as poor sleep increases hunger hormones significantly. Crash diets rarely work long-term.

Q: How do I get better sleep?
A: Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, including weekends (most important factor). Keep your bedroom cool, dark, and quiet. Avoid screens for at least 30 minutes before bed (blue light suppresses melatonin). Avoid caffeine after 2pm. Avoid large meals and alcohol within 3 hours of bedtime. If you cannot sleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something calm in dim light rather than lying awake anxiously. Exercise regularly but not within 2 hours of bedtime. Keep your phone outside the bedroom.

Q: How do I prevent lower back pain from sitting at a desk all day?
A: Set up your workstation correctly: screen at eye level, chair adjusted so feet are flat on the floor, hips slightly higher than knees, lower back supported. Take a 5-minute standing or walking break every 45 to 60 minutes. Strengthen your core muscles (planks, bridges, bird-dogs) as core strength directly supports the lower back. Stretch the hip flexors daily as they tighten from prolonged sitting and pull the pelvis out of alignment, causing back pain. Avoid sitting with your wallet in your back pocket as it tilts the pelvis.

Q: How do I calculate how many calories I should eat per day?
A: Start with your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): for men, approximately 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age + 5. For women, the same formula minus 161 instead of +5. Multiply by your activity level: 1.2 for sedentary, 1.375 for lightly active (1-3 days exercise per week), 1.55 for moderately active (3-5 days), 1.725 for very active (6-7 days). The result is your maintenance calorie level. Subtract 500 calories per day for approximately 0.5kg loss per week.

Q: What is the best way to stay hydrated during exercise?
A: Drink 500ml of water about 2 hours before exercise. During exercise, drink 150 to 250ml every 15 to 20 minutes. After exercise, drink 500ml to 1 liter to replace what was lost. Signs of dehydration during exercise: dark yellow urine, headache, dizziness, and reduced performance. For exercise longer than 1 hour in hot conditions, electrolyte drinks (containing sodium, potassium, and magnesium) are more effective than plain water at preventing cramps and fatigue.

SECTION 3: SKIN, HAIR AND GROOMING
======================================

Q: How do I treat acne?
A: Wash your face twice daily with a gentle cleanser (not soap). Do not scrub or pop pimples as this causes scarring and spreads bacteria. Use non-comedogenic (won't block pores) moisturizer. Effective over-the-counter treatments: salicylic acid (2%) for blackheads and whiteheads, benzoyl peroxide (2.5% to 5%) for inflamed pimples. Change your pillowcase twice weekly. Keep hair off your face. Avoid touching your face. Diet: some people find that reducing dairy and high-glycemic foods (white bread, sugar) reduces breakouts. See a dermatologist for moderate to severe acne.

Q: How do I treat dry and flaky skin?
A: Moisturize immediately after showering while skin is still slightly damp to lock in moisture. Use a thick cream or ointment rather than a thin lotion for very dry skin. Look for ingredients like hyaluronic acid, glycerin, shea butter, or ceramides. Use lukewarm (not hot) water for showers and baths. Avoid harsh soaps or over-washing. Use a humidifier in dry climates or air-conditioned rooms. Drink adequate water. Omega-3 fatty acids from fish, flaxseed, and walnuts also help skin retain moisture from the inside.

Q: How do I prevent and treat dandruff?
A: Use an anti-dandruff shampoo containing selenium sulfide, zinc pyrithione, or ketoconazole. Leave the shampoo in for 5 minutes before rinsing. Use 2 to 3 times per week. Rinse hair thoroughly as residue can cause irritation. Tea tree oil added to your regular shampoo has antifungal properties that can help. Manage stress as it is a common dandruff trigger. Avoid scratching the scalp. If dandruff is severe, reddish, or spreading beyond the scalp, see a dermatologist as it may be seborrheic dermatitis requiring prescription treatment.

Q: How do I treat sunburned lips?
A: Apply aloe vera gel or petroleum jelly (Vaseline) to soothe and moisturize. Do not lick your lips as saliva evaporates and leaves them drier. Stay hydrated. Avoid further sun exposure and wear a lip balm with SPF when outdoors. Ibuprofen can help reduce inflammation and pain. Do not pick or peel skin from the lips. Healing takes 3 to 7 days. To prevent: always use a lip balm with SPF 30 or higher when outdoors, especially near water, snow, or sand which reflect UV radiation.

SECTION 4: MENTAL HEALTH AND WELLBEING
======================================

Q: How do I manage stress and anxiety?
A: Breathing techniques work immediately: try box breathing (inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4). Physical exercise reduces cortisol (stress hormone) and releases endorphins. Progressive muscle relaxation: tense and release each muscle group from feet to head. Identify your stress triggers and make a plan to address them. Limit alcohol and caffeine (both worsen anxiety). Maintain a regular sleep schedule. Social support is one of the most powerful stress buffers: talk to someone you trust. Seek professional help if anxiety is affecting your daily life.

Q: How do I stop feeling homesick when travelling?
A: Stay connected with family and friends through video calls when internet is available (schedule regular call times). Bring a small comfort item from home. Establish a familiar routine at your new location (same wake-up time, familiar food occasionally). Explore and make your new environment feel familiar by getting to know your neighborhood. Join groups or activities to meet people. Be patient with yourself: homesickness is normal and usually improves after 2 to 4 weeks. Write in a journal about both home and your new experiences.

Q: How do I deal with travel loneliness?
A: Join group tours, classes, or activities (cooking classes, surfing lessons, group hikes) where you will naturally meet other travelers. Stay in social accommodations like hostels even if you prefer private rooms (use common areas). Visit cafes, markets, and local hangout spots rather than staying in your room. Use apps to find other solo travelers or locals interested in meeting up. Say yes to invitations from other travelers. Remember that discomfort in social situations improves with practice and the effort is always worth it.
