COOKING AND FOOD OFFLINE GUIDE
================================
Recipes, cooking techniques, and food knowledge for when you have no internet.

======================================
SECTION 1: BASIC RECIPES WITHOUT SPECIAL EQUIPMENT
======================================

Q: How do I make a basic omelette?
A: Beat 2 to 3 eggs in a bowl with a pinch of salt and pepper. Heat a non-stick pan over medium heat, add a small knob of butter or oil. Pour in the egg mixture and tilt the pan so it spreads evenly. As the edges set, use a spatula to gently pull them toward the center while tilting the pan. When the top is just barely set (still slightly glossy), add any fillings (cheese, vegetables, ham) to one half. Fold the other half over the fillings. Slide onto a plate. Total time: 3 minutes.

Q: How do I make instant noodles taste much better?
A: Do not add all the sodium powder packet (half is usually enough salt). Add an egg directly into the boiling broth and stir to create ribbons. Add vegetables: frozen peas, corn, spinach, or sliced mushrooms. Add soy sauce, sesame oil, or chili paste for depth. A spoonful of peanut butter stirred in creates a satay-style broth. Top with fresh green onions, sesame seeds, or a lime wedge. Fry some garlic and ginger in oil first before adding water for a dramatically richer base.

Q: How do I make a simple fried rice?
A: Use day-old cold rice (freshly cooked rice is too wet and sticks together). Heat oil in a pan over high heat. Add garlic and any vegetables, stir-fry for 2 minutes. Push everything to the side, crack 2 eggs in the center and scramble them. Mix in the cold rice, breaking up any clumps. Add soy sauce (2 tablespoons), sesame oil (1 teaspoon), and white pepper. Stir-fry everything together for 3 to 4 minutes until the rice is hot and slightly crispy. Add any protein (cooked chicken, shrimp, spam) last.

Q: How do I make a simple pasta sauce from canned tomatoes?
A: Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Fry 4 cloves of crushed garlic for 1 minute until fragrant. Add one 400g can of crushed tomatoes. Add a pinch of sugar (reduces acidity), salt, pepper, and dried oregano or basil. Simmer on low heat for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens. For richness, stir in a tablespoon of butter at the end. Toss with cooked pasta and top with grated cheese. This is a complete, restaurant-quality meal from pantry staples.

Q: How do I cook the perfect hard-boiled egg?
A: Place eggs in a single layer in a pot. Cover with cold water by 2 cm. Bring to a rolling boil over high heat. Once boiling, cover the pot, remove from heat, and let stand for: 6 minutes for runny yolk, 8 minutes for jammy yolk, 10 minutes for fully set but still creamy yolk, 12 minutes for firm hard boiled. Transfer immediately to a bowl of ice water for 5 minutes to stop cooking and make peeling easy. Starting with cold water and bringing to a boil gives more consistent results than dropping eggs into boiling water.

Q: How do I make chai (masala tea) from scratch?
A: Crush together: 4 cardamom pods, 2 cloves, a small piece of cinnamon, and a small slice of ginger. Add these spices to 1.5 cups of water and bring to a boil. Add 2 teaspoons of black tea leaves (or 2 tea bags). Simmer for 2 minutes. Add 0.5 cup of whole milk. Simmer for another 2 minutes. Strain into cups and sweeten with sugar to taste. The secret is using whole milk and letting the tea simmer (not just steep) with the milk to develop a rich, creamy flavor.

Q: How do I make a simple vegetable soup?
A: In a large pot, heat oil over medium heat and fry one diced onion until soft. Add 2 cloves of garlic and fry for 1 minute. Add any vegetables you have: carrots, potatoes, beans, peas, spinach (cut into bite-size pieces). Cover with water or vegetable stock (4 to 6 cups). Add salt, pepper, and a bay leaf. Bring to a boil then simmer for 20 to 30 minutes until vegetables are tender. Stir in a small can of tomatoes for richness. Blend half the soup for a thicker texture if desired. Fresh herbs at the end brighten the flavour.

SECTION 2: COOKING TECHNIQUES AND TIPS
======================================

Q: How do I know when oil is hot enough for frying?
A: Insert the handle of a wooden spoon or a wooden chopstick into the oil. If small bubbles form around the wood immediately, the oil is ready (around 160 to 170°C). For a drop of water test: flick a small drop of water into the oil. If it sizzles, the oil is ready. Do not use more water than a tiny drop as it can cause dangerous splattering. Never leave hot oil unattended. If oil begins to smoke, it is too hot and approaching its smoke point; remove from heat and let it cool slightly.

Q: How do I save a meal that has too much salt?
A: Add starch: a raw potato cut into large pieces absorbs salt as it cooks (remove before serving). Add more unsalted ingredients to dilute the saltiness. Add acid: a squeeze of lemon juice or a splash of vinegar can balance saltiness. Add sweetness: a pinch of sugar counteracts salt. For soups, add more water or unsalted stock. For sauces, add more tomatoes, coconut milk, or cream. Unfortunately, there is no perfect fix; dilution and balance are the only real options.

Q: How do I make meat tender when cooking?
A: Marinate tough cuts in an acid-based marinade (yogurt, vinegar, citrus juice, buttermilk) for several hours or overnight. The acid begins to break down muscle fibres. Slow cooking at low temperature for several hours makes even the toughest cuts fork-tender (braising). Let meat rest for 5 to 10 minutes after cooking before cutting to allow juices to redistribute. Cut cooked meat against the grain (perpendicular to the muscle fibres) for maximum tenderness. Pound tougher cuts with a meat mallet before cooking.

Q: How do I stop onions from making me cry when cutting them?
A: Chill the onion in the refrigerator for 30 minutes before cutting (cold temperature slows the release of the irritating gas). Cut near a running tap or under your range hood fan. Use a very sharp knife as blunt knives crush cells and release more gas. Do not cut the root end of the onion until last as it has the highest concentration of irritants. Breathe through your mouth. Wearing contact lenses helps as they provide a barrier for the eyes.

Q: What is the difference between baking soda and baking powder?
A: Baking soda (bicarbonate of soda) is a pure raising agent that needs an acid to activate it (buttermilk, yogurt, vinegar, lemon juice, honey). It reacts immediately so bake right after mixing. Baking powder already contains an acid and a starch; it only needs moisture and heat to activate. Use it when a recipe has no acidic ingredient. They are not interchangeable: 1 teaspoon baking powder equals about 1/4 teaspoon baking soda (you also need an acidic ingredient when using baking soda).

Q: How do I store food to prevent waste?
A: Store herbs in a glass of water like flowers (lasts a week in the fridge). Wrap cut avocado tightly in cling film directly against the flesh. Store tomatoes at room temperature (never in the fridge, it kills flavour). Keep bananas away from other fruit (they emit ethylene gas which ripens other fruits faster). Store bread in a cool, dry place or freeze it. Keep mushrooms in a paper bag not plastic (plastic traps moisture and causes rapid rotting). Wrap leafy greens in a dry paper towel inside a bag (the paper absorbs excess moisture).

SECTION 3: FOOD ALLERGIES AND DIETARY REQUIREMENTS
======================================

Q: What foods contain gluten that I should avoid with gluten intolerance?
A: Gluten is found in: wheat (bread, pasta, flour, couscous, bulgur, semolina), barley (malt, barley flour, beer), rye (rye bread, rye crackers), and some oats (due to cross-contamination unless labeled gluten-free). Hidden sources include soy sauce (contains wheat; use tamari instead), many processed foods, soups with flour thickeners, marinades, dressings, and some medications. Safe grains include: rice, corn (maize), quinoa, buckwheat, millet, oats labeled certified gluten-free, and potato flour.

Q: What foods are safe for a peanut allergy?
A: Avoid: peanuts in any form (roasted, boiled, peanut butter, peanut oil, groundnut oil). Hidden sources include: some Asian sauces and curries (satay, pad Thai), some chocolate and confectionery, baked goods, cereals, vegetarian meat substitutes, and some salad dressings. Note: peanuts are legumes, not tree nuts, so tree nut allergy and peanut allergy are separate conditions. Always carry an EpiPen if prescribed. Read food labels carefully. When eating out, inform the restaurant staff clearly about the allergy severity.

Q: What are high-protein foods for vegetarians?
A: Excellent vegetarian protein sources: lentils (18g per cup cooked), chickpeas (15g per cup), black beans (15g per cup), tofu (20g per cup), tempeh (31g per cup), Greek yogurt (17g per 170g), cottage cheese (25g per cup), eggs (6g each), quinoa (8g per cup, a complete protein), hemp seeds (10g per 3 tablespoons), edamame (17g per cup), and peanut butter (8g per 2 tablespoons). Combining different plant proteins throughout the day ensures you get all essential amino acids.

Q: What should someone with high blood pressure eat less of?
A: Reduce sodium (salt): aim for less than 2,300mg per day (about 1 teaspoon). High-sodium foods to limit: processed and packaged foods, canned soups, soy sauce, deli meats, pickles, fast food, chips and salty snacks. Increase: potassium-rich foods (bananas, potatoes, spinach, beans) which counteract sodium. Reduce alcohol to no more than one drink per day. The DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean protein, and low-fat dairy while limiting red meat, sweets, and saturated fats.

SECTION 4: DRINKS AND BEVERAGES
======================================

Q: What is the difference between espresso, americano, latte, and cappuccino?
A: Espresso: 30ml of concentrated coffee brewed by forcing hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure. The base of all espresso drinks.
Americano: Espresso diluted with hot water (1 part espresso + 2 to 3 parts water). Similar strength to drip coffee but different flavour.
Latte: Espresso with steamed milk (1 part espresso + 3 to 4 parts steamed milk + thin foam layer). Mild coffee flavour, creamy texture.
Cappuccino: Espresso with equal parts steamed milk and thick milk foam (1:1:1 ratio). Stronger coffee flavour than latte, drier/airier texture.
Flat White: Similar to latte but smaller (1 part espresso + 2 parts micro-foam milk). Stronger coffee to milk ratio than latte.

Q: How many cups of coffee per day is safe?
A: Most health authorities consider up to 400mg of caffeine per day safe for healthy adults. This equals approximately 4 cups of regular drip coffee, or 2 to 3 shots of espresso, or 8 cups of regular tea. Pregnant women should limit to 200mg per day. People sensitive to caffeine may experience anxiety, insomnia, and rapid heartbeat at lower doses. Caffeine has a half-life of about 5 to 6 hours, meaning half of the caffeine from your 4pm coffee is still in your system at 10pm.

Q: What is the alcohol content of common drinks?
A: Beer: typically 4 to 5% alcohol (standard can/bottle = about 1.5 units). Wine: typically 11 to 14% alcohol (standard glass 175ml = about 2 units). Spirits (whisky, vodka, gin, rum): typically 40% alcohol (25ml measure = 1 unit). One unit of alcohol = 10ml or 8g of pure alcohol. Low-risk drinking guidelines: no more than 14 units per week, spread over at least 3 days, with at least 2 alcohol-free days. Drinking water between alcoholic drinks slows consumption and reduces hangover severity.

Q: How do I make oral rehydration solution at home?
A: Dissolve in 1 liter of clean water: 6 level teaspoons of sugar (table sugar or glucose) and half a level teaspoon of salt. This approximates the World Health Organization standard ORS formula. Alternatively, add a pinch of salt to a glass of coconut water or diluted fruit juice. Drink in small, frequent sips if vomiting is present. This is an emergency hydration measure for diarrhoea, vomiting, or heat exhaustion when commercial ORS packets are not available.
